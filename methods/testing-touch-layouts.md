# Testing touch layouts

## Use when
- Testing touch targets + layouts under real grip + real use context.
- 5 points: where to observe · what not to ask · whose devices · what screens sense · what to design for.

## Steps
1. **Observe in realistic contexts**, e.g. phone pulled from pocket, relaxed viewing. Why: lab settings → unnaturally focused attention, unlike real touch use.
2. **Do not ask people how they hold their phone.** Why: people cannot report grip accurately + switch grips constantly.
3. **Test on users' own devices**, not team's phones.
4. **Remember what screens sense**: touch screens blind to pressure, except in drawing tools + games.
5. **Design for varied grips, contexts + errors**, not one ideal path.

## Misreading
- Wrong: design for one ideal touch path. → Right: this = happy-path bias, one-best-way assumption inherited from time-and-motion study, critiqued for this; design for varied grips, contexts + errors.

## Hypothetical example
Hypothetical: team testing parking-payment app has participants pay on own phones beside parked car while holding shopping bags, not at desk w/ team phone; layout then planned for one-handed use, two-handed use + mis-taps.

## Pitfalls
- Lab-only sessions → unnaturally focused attention, unlike real touch use.
- Asking about grip → people cannot report it accurately.
- Team devices only → test on users' own.
- Pressure-dependent interactions outside drawing tools + games → screens cannot sense pressure.
