# Team design principles

## Use when
- Turning UX laws into design principles team can apply.
- Checking principle-to-law mapping does not overstate law.
- Covers: 7 authoring steps, what makes principle usable, keeping it in use, cautions on mapping to laws.

## Steps (7)
Source: one insurance-software startup, 2023 case.
1. **Pick authors**: product + engineering required; founders included.
2. **Share reading in advance.**
3. **Each author brainstorms alone.**
4. **Group review + vote.**
5. **Merge overlaps + tighten wording.**
6. **Link each principle to supporting law.**
7. **Document principles + add to onboarding.**
- Evidence: live group writing of principles beat asynchronous homework.

## What makes a principle usable
|Quality|Check question|
|---|---|
|Direct|Says plainly what to do?|
|Actionable|Can someone act on it in next design decision?|
|Answers real question|Settles real question team faces?|
|Takes priority stance|Says what wins when two goods conflict?|
|Memorable|Can people recall it w/o looking up?|

- Hypothetical: fails → "Our banking app should feel effortless" (no stance, nothing to act on).
- Hypothetical: passes → "In money transfers, show the fee before the confirm button, even if it slows the flow" (direct, actionable, picks side).

## Keeping principles in use
- Symptom: principles living only in wiki get forgotten.
- Fix: cite them in requirements, stand-ups + retrospectives.
- Illustrate w/ frequent small interactions (modals, alerts, inputs, wording), not headline features.

## Mapping principles to laws: cautions
- Mapping can stretch law past what it says.
- Wrong: Fitts's Law justifies color contrast. → Right: stretched mapping; present loose principle-to-law mappings as related practices, not as what law establishes.
- House rules (e.g. ≤ 3 options at once; explanations under 80 characters) = internal choices, not research thresholds.
