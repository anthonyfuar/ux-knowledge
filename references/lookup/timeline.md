# Timeline

Research behind UX laws + foundational works of humane design, by date; each row: what appeared/got shown + (where known) what paper/book behind it established.

## Use when
- Dating study/concept; seeing what finding came first; checking year before citing.
- Citing idea's source work, or pointing someone to further reading.
- Span: UX-law research runs 1890 to 2005; foundational papers + books extend chronology to 2020.

## How to cite from this file
1. Before quoting year → check misquoted-claims.md for known misattributions + misdatings.
2. Works described, not titled. If foundational work's exact title uncertain → describe it (year, kind, what it established), not name it.
3. Law each event belongs to → ../laws/index.md (30 UX laws); each law's origin + evidence in its own file.
4. Shared background of Gestalt rows → ../laws/gestalt-principles-overview.md.

|1935–1936|Findings from prefrontal ablation + delayed-response experiments|
|1943|Book first proposed mental models|

|1950|Vigilance research|

|1953|Second reaction-time study, behind Hick-Hyman Law|
|1953|Cocktail party attention studies|
|1954|Recoding demonstration, later reported in 1956 paper behind Miller's Law|
|1954|Motor-capacity study behind Fitts's Law: paper analysed aimed movements of varying length in information terms|

|1956|Paper on how much people can tell apart + recall at once: source of Miller's Law + term chunking|
|1958|Filter Theory of attention|
|1960|Attenuation Model of attention|
|1962|Report: technology's job = amplify human comprehension + problem-solving (intelligence augmentation)|
|1963|Late Selection Theory of attention|
|1968|Short-term store model of memory|
|1970|Book coined word overchoice, root of choice-overload idea|
|1972|Joint paper: people rate how likely something seems by resemblance to typical case; first introduction of cognitive biases|
|1973|Capacity model of attention|
|1975|Term flow coined|
|1975|Argument: Gestalt grouping laws = learned heuristics|
|1977|Goal-gradient research review|
|1980|Paper on practice + power law of learning: basis for comparing designs by learning curves + plateaus|
|1981|Framing effect, from joint paper (later in 1982 heuristics-and-biases volume)|
|1982|IBM paper argued for sub-second computer response times, behind Doherty Threshold. Caveat: 400 ms figure tied to it = later attribution|
|1982|Edited volume collected heuristics-and-biases research|
|1984|Training-wheels study|
|1984|Field study of text-editor use|
|1985|Cards supporting learners' guided exploration|
|Mid-1980s|Conservation of complexity, behind Tesler's Law|
|1987|Paradox of the Active User, from chapter in edited volume on cognitive side of human-computer interaction|
|1988|Cognitive load theory: paper gave formal statement + linked it to instructional design|
|1988|Book set out seven stages of action, introduced conceptual model as design idea; four principles bridging gulfs of execution + evaluation: visibility, conceptual model, mappings, feedback|
|1989|Rule-discovery study: paper compared reasoning across two tasks, discovering rule vs evaluating hypotheses; evidence reasoning strategy depends on task; not proof of complexity bias|
|1990|Book showed same grouping logic applies to sound unfolding over time (auditory scene analysis)|
|1992|Common region proposed|
|1993|Cold-water experiment: paper found adding gentler final stretch to unpleasant episode can make people favour it over alternative hurting less overall; part of Peak-End Rule evidence|
|1994|Uniform connectedness proposed|
|1995|ATM study|
|1996|Medical-procedure study: paper compared pain patients rated in real time vs looking back, across two minimally invasive procedures; confirmed peak-end pattern in medicine|
|1998|Edited volume: case studies on minimalist, task-oriented instruction for active users|
|1999|Theory linking positive affect + dopamine|
|1999|Vision-science textbook synthesized grouping principles, incl. common region|
|2000|Jakob's Law stated|
|2000|Reward-program work|
|2000|Paper argued ending = boundary defining episode's peak → explains why endings carry extra weight in remembered experience|
|2003|Randomized trial w/ colonoscopy patients, same research line as 1996 procedure study: better ending changed how patients remembered procedure + their return rate|
|2004|Emotional design book: three response levels: visceral, behavioral, reflective. Holds positive affect broadens thinking + raises problem tolerance = affective route of Aesthetic-Usability Effect|
|2004|Book argued beyond certain point, more options reduce satisfaction|
|2005|Feature-fatigue experiments|
|2006|Interaction-design book w/ best-known interview on conservation of complexity (Tesler's Law). Argument: when complexity left in to spare builder, users pay instead; trade scale: one engineer-week to remove it vs one minute lost per day by each of a million users|
|2010|Book placed perceived complexity in user's conceptual model; holds simpler use requires more complex mechanism underneath|
|2011|Popular book on System 1, System 2 + their biases|
|2020|Book: bias shapes user behavior, stakeholder decisions + team dynamics; argues for more humane products|
|2020|Paper: task-based taxonomy of cognitive biases in visualization: 6 user tasks (estimation, decision, hypothesis assessment, causal attribution, recall, opinion reporting) + 5 bias flavors (association, baseline, inertia, outcome, self-perspective); supports bias checklist for dashboards|
