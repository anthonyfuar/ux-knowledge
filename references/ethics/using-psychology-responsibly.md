Guardrails for using any psychological law/lever w/o exploiting people; levers flagged ethically sensitive; well-being cases to copy.

## Use when
- Law/pattern used to raise engagement, on any surface in [pattern index](../patterns/index.md).
- Feature goals may conflict w/ user goals.

## Stance
- Bend design to fit people; never bend people to fit product.
- Design, however elegant, can fail whenever it cuts across how people make sense of + take in surroundings.
- One body of psychological insight, two possible design outcomes: compulsive products preying on mind, or easily grasped products.
- Instead of copying pattern → use psychological laws to explain why it works.
- Use laws to give stakeholders defensible rationale for design decision.
- Fitts's Law, Jakob's Law + Hick's Law = predictive models; most other UX laws = lenses for looking at design.

## The risk
- Same psychological knowledge improving products can serve to exploit minds → anxiety, captured attention, lost sleep, weaker relationships.
- Citation caveat: same findings making products intuitive can also serve to exploit attention.
- Platform craft of holding attention rising steadily.
- Once product begins shifting people's self-image + how they form relationships → damage counts as serious.

## Guardrails
1. Openly admit risk of exploiting minds exists.
2. Before using law to raise engagement → ask whether goal matches user's goal.
3. If feature's goals conflict w/ users' goals → question whether feature should exist at all.
4. Usage-metric blind spots: log behaviour only, never product's effect on person's life or people's motives → fill gap: talk w/ users in person, not via screen, + qualitative research on product's effect on their lives.
5. Define success by value delivered, not time spent.
6. Target test: fail = screen-only payoffs + exchanges start replacing people's lived experience; pass = technology adds to lived experience.

## Ethically sensitive levers
|Lever|How it acts on people|Guardrail|Flagged in|
|---|---|---|---|
|Sub-400 ms responsiveness|Apps responding in under 400 ms called 'addicting'|Weigh speed-driven engagement vs well-being|../laws/doherty-threshold.md|
|Artificial progress, artificial head starts|Work on people|Use only w/o deception; inflated/fake progress = flagged lever|../laws/goal-gradient-effect.md, ../laws/zeigarnik-effect.md|
|Deliberately added delays|↑|Use only w/o deception|../laws/doherty-threshold.md|
|Emphasis, grouping, defaults|Steer choices; most people keep preset default|Steer only toward user's own goal; preset option most users would choose if fully informed; defaults visibly changeable|—|
|Retention via self-serving defaults + loss-framed guilt copy|Exploit loss aversion; can backfire as reactance|Avoid both; switching + cancelling as easy as sign-up; when migrating people to redesign → explain concrete gains + offer temporary way back|—|
|Near-goal window|People near goal become less price-sensitive + more open to promotions. 2006 bonus-miles survey: 56% vs 38% accepted weekly marketing emails for bonus miles, higher share = those close to goal|Never use near-goal window to extract consent, raise prices or lock users in|—|

## Well-being cases to copy
- Google announced its Digital Wellbeing tools at Google I/O.
  - Aims: healthy habits; switching off when person needs to; attention on what counts; clearer view of own usage.
  - Means: Family Link (parent-set rules), finer notification control, usage dashboard.
- Facebook accepted slightly less time in its app by reordering feed so friends + family posts rank first, because its success measure became 'meaningful interactions' instead of time on site.

## Check questions
- Lever's goal matches user's goal?
- Feature's goals conflict w/ users' goals? If yes → should it exist?
- Beyond usage data, what did talking to users + qualitative research show about effects on their lives?
- Default preset to what most users would choose if fully informed, + visibly changeable?
- Leaving/switching as easy as signing up?
- Anyone asked for consent, charged more or locked in while near goal?
- Success measured by value delivered, not time spent?

## Hypothetical examples
- Hypothetical (loss-framed guilt copy): meal-kit cancel screen tells member their family will miss out on healthy dinners because of this choice → retention by guilt + loss framing; replace w/ neutral confirmation.
- Hypothetical: hiking app moves route planner in redesign → screen lists what got faster + 'use old planner' switch for limited period.
- Hypothetical: savings app waits until member close to savings goal, then offers higher-fee account locking funds for fixed term → near-goal window used to raise price + lock in; do not time offers to it.
