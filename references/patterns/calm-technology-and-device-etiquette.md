# Calm technology and device etiquette

## Use when
- Covers: peripheral attention, quiet signals, staying out of way, graceful failure, device manners, calm technology's origin + scope.
- Open when designing/reviewing: ambient status, audio + visual signals, transitions, assistants, widgets, connected devices, failure states, usability tests, device business model.
- Scope: anything claiming place in someone's day, not itself what they came for.
- Principles served: Respectful, Empowering, Intentional, Resilient, Inclusive. Respectful = most-served here.

## What calm means
- Aim = attention handed back to room + its people; not quieter device.
- Calm-technology argument: gain = how far people can be genuinely present w/ each other, beyond objects around them.
- Calm = fit between product + attention its purpose deserves; not one universal setting for every product.
- If built to thrill (game, live event, drama) → has earned centre of attention; quieting it would empty its point.
- Calm test: attention taken matches thing's purpose?
- Hypothetical: hospital-ward infusion pump alarms loudly when line blocked → passes. Fitness app, same alarm for new badge → fails.

## Origin + eight principles
- Term calm technology coined in 1990s; later expanded into eight principles:

|#|Calm-technology principle|
|---|---|
|1|Require the least attention possible|
|2|Inform + create calm|
|3|Make use of the periphery of attention|
|4|Amplify the best of humans + the best of machines|
|5|Communicate w/o speaking|
|6|Work even when failing|
|7|Use the minimum amount of technology|
|8|Respect social norms|

### Should this status sit at the edge of attention or the centre? (design for the periphery)
- When: people could sense status / ambient info rather than read it.
- Do: place at attention's edge → noticed only when it changes. Easily promoted to centre + demoted back. Add to someone's periphery only when it raises their knowledge + ability to act.
- Avoid: symbol dashboards needing interpretation; can only occupy centre, never periphery.
- Why: Respectful. Selective Attention = limited attention peripheral signals work within.
- Hypothetical: thin light strip above warehouse packing bench brightens slightly as order queue grows; packers register it only when it shifts.
- Hypothetical: bike-share dock ticks softly, steadily while locking; rhythm changes when bike not seated properly; only change noticed.

### How loud should the signal be? (whisper, do not shout)
- When: choosing audio + visual signals.
- Do: subtle, meaningful tones + lights. Loud, abrupt alerts only for genuine emergencies = status worth shouting about, e.g. smoke alarm. People control signal volume.
- Avoid: shrill beeps on every touch w/ no way to silence them.
- Why: Respectful.
- Hypothetical: library self-checkout kiosk confirms returned book w/ short soft chime, not spoken announcement.

### How should a non-crisis event appear? (inform, do not alarm)
- When: non-crisis event needs conveying.
- Do: non-crisis alerts appear smoothly, w/o taking over display. Alert colour, size + style customizable.
- Avoid: flashing lights + intensely bright screens for non-crisis events.
- Why: Respectful.
- Hypothetical (anti-pattern): office printer strobes red light across open floor whenever paper tray runs low.
- Hypothetical: fabric-covered speaker in clinic waiting room shows status as soft dots glowing through cloth; blends in, still readable.

### When should the product ask for opt-ins? (ask, do not assume)
- When: opt-in choices front-loaded at setup, before value clear to person.
- Do: offer relevant notifications + next steps when they become meaningful, not up front.
- Avoid: wall of permission toggles on first launch.
- Why: Intentional.
- Hypothetical: right after resident books bulky-waste pickup, city-services app offers reminder for evening before collection.

### How should one state change into another? (gradient softening)
- When: any transition between states, colours, volumes / brightness levels.
- Do: fade notifications in, not abruptly. Ramp audio gradually. Lights brighten slowly in morning, dim in evening. Indicator hues shift cool → warm across day.
- Avoid: notification flashing on at full brightness. Harsh colour divisions → eye drawn to colours, not data.
- Why: Respectful.
- Hypothetical: care-home hallway night-light rises slowly to low glow when resident gets up; no snapping on.
- Hypothetical: car cabin lights dim gradually after doors close; no cutting out.

### How much status to show, and in what form? (communicate w/o speaking, show the minimum)
- When: conveying status.
- Do: prefer tones, lights + haptics over voice / text. Show only status needed now. Speak only when it truly adds value.
- Avoid: appliance narrating its every action. Screen dense w/ numbers nobody reads.
- Why: Inclusive.
- Hypothetical: e-bike shows battery as one coloured handlebar ring; no numeric readout during ride.

### Should awareness between people be pushed or offered? (offer, never demand)
- When: designing awareness channels between people.
- Do: partial separation + optional clues → each individual decides what moves to their attention's centre.
- Avoid: always-on microphones / auto-joined rooms obligating people's attention.
- Why: Empowering.
- Hypothetical (demanding channel): family location app alerts all members each time anyone leaves home → every movement forced into everyone's attention centre.
- Hypothetical: tutoring platform shows tutor availability dot students can glance at, vs pop-up chat request requiring acceptance.

### Should the assistant or widget be visible right now? (invisible until needed)
- When: assistants, widgets, ambient products.
- Do: stay out of way while still extending what person can do. Appear when summoned, recede after. Ask whether task needs screen at all; if not → glanceable, ambient / conversational routes. Count attention cost of device's mere presence, not only its use.
- Avoid: assistants surfacing suggestions to prove they work.
- Why: Empowering.
- Misreadings: Wrong: shrinking face-mounted hardware = automatically calmer. Wrong: early natural-language personal devices = solved case of invisible-until-needed → do not cite them as one.
- Screenless personal devices:
  - Hypothetical: pocket travel companion, no display: traveller asks about gates + bookings in everyday speech; its software shapes itself around this one traveller; trip data stays on device, under traveller's control.
  - Their ambition (removing screens, not adding them) = sound.
  - Far less app reliance + far fewer display hours: what stronger AI could often make possible.
  - Limit: today's natural-language personal devices cover only small share of phone's jobs; category still first-wave + unfinished.
  - Caveat: direction of travel, not answer available now.
- Hypothetical: pill organizer whose lid lights only when dose due.
- Hypothetical: help assistant in workplace expense tool stays small icon until someone types question; closes once answer used.

### How should a background product change over time? (earn invisibility through familiarity)
- When: product meant to recede into background of someone's day.
- Do: keep layout, controls + signal meanings stable long enough to learn. Change in small, deliberate steps. Old route works while people adjust. Redesign must earn its disruption.
- Avoid: reworking everything at once for novelty. Blocking use behind update. Moving familiar controls every release.
- Why: Respectful (also Inclusive). Nothing stops taking attention until people can use it w/o thought → familiarity lets product recede. Usability side: conventions → interface recedes; redesigns opt-in + reversible (see departing from convention).
- Hypothetical: transit-card reader's green flash = "fare accepted" at every station for years → riders never look twice.

### What still works when supports fail? (work even when failing)
- When: battery dies, server down, network slow / person errs.
- Do: usable baseline still working when supporting systems fail. Offline + low-speed modes. Hand off to human w/ person's account details.
- Avoid: smart device bricked when offline. Chatbot loop w/ no exit.
- Why: Resilient.
- Case: connected pet feeder left animals unfed when its servers failed; no offline behaviour built.
- Hypothetical: smart door lock still opens w/ physical key when battery flat.

### Where should usability tests run? (test under sub-optimal conditions)
- When: usability testing.
- Do: test in noise, glare, motion, low battery, poor connectivity + socially awkward settings.
- Avoid: test sessions only in quiet conference room.
- Why: Resilient.
- Hypothetical (under-tested): railway ticket machine tested only indoors; touchscreen washes out in direct sunlight on platform.
- Hypothetical: parking-payment app's release checklist includes paying in rain, w/ gloves on, at dusk.

### Which business model fits a device in someone's life? (revenue from being beloved, not from attention)
- When: choosing business model, esp. home devices.
- Do: prefer models not dependent on maximizing engagement. People pay for well-mannered products.
- Avoid: attention-based revenue → needs constant engagement + hot emotions → structurally opposed to device etiquette.
- Why: Intentional.
- Hypothetical: kitchen-scale maker earns from one-time hardware sales, sells no ad slots → companion app has no feed built to keep owners scrolling.
