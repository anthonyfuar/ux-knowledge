# Cross-cutting practices

## Use when
- Team + process habits for every humane-design principle; each w/ Do + Why.
- Setting up design reviews, kickoffs, research, metrics, team norms.
- Concern spans all seven principles, not only one.

## Practices

### Setting up a design review: which lens? (seven principles as review lens)
- Do: design-review template, one row per principle; each = definition + best practices (from [Principles index](index.md)).
- Do: each decision: check related principles too, not only one applied.
- Why: shared, named criteria make well-being something review can check, not vague aspiration.

### Before ideating: where does the product touch human sensitivities? (human-sensitivities assessment)
1. Record where product exploits vs respects human sensitivities, e.g. social approval, reciprocity, variable reward, fear of missing out + similar.
2. Ideate in second pass, after sensitivities recorded.
- Why: well-intentioned teams reach for manipulative patterns unless review names them.

### Could the product be used to harm? (misuse + harm brainstorming)
- Ask: could product aggravate depression/addiction, exclude groups, provoke oversharing, enable stalking/blackmail/control?
- Step: research how harms feel to those experiencing them.
- Step: quantify each harm's current incidence (how often now).
- Why: well-intended products cause unintended harm; naming harm scenarios early = cheaper than crisis handling.
- Method → ../methods/misuse-case-brainstorming.md.

### Project kickoff: which harms + which incentives? (harm-and-incentive map at kickoff)
- Step: list physical, emotional + societal harms product could cause + incentive possibly driving each.
- Do: set team principles at kickoff → keep them when later tempted.
- Why: unethical outcomes usually come from short-term goals crowding out trust, not bad intent.

### Hearing a trade-off phrase? (watchlist of trade-off language)
- Watchlist: "just an edge case", "most of our users", "only a test", "it's in the terms", "everyone else does it".
- If one comes up → stop, re-examine team motives.
- Why: such phrases make harm feel temporary, or as if people already consented.

### Reporting a test or an edge case: percentages or people? (count people, not percentages)
- Do: express tests + so-called edge cases as absolute headcounts.
- Avoid: shipping to test cohort anything you wouldn't keep permanently.
- Why: 1% of large platform's people = millions of humans.

### Researching: what do people give up? (research what people give up)
- Step: study context of use + product's effect on relationships.
- Step: find what people sacrifice to use product.
- Step: talk face to face w/ people on product's mental effects.
- Step: whole team watches real sessions.
- Limit: task-completion tests show speed, not whether people wanted outcome or its cost to them.

### Choosing metrics: what keeps them honest? (metrics w/ conscience)
- Do: pair each business KPI w/ user-goal metric + counter-metric, e.g. regret, compulsive re-opening, complaints/abuse.
- Do: quantify unhealthy behaviours → they get measured.
- Do: give designers standing to refuse metric-driven tricks.
- Why: cultures built around single metric manufacture deceptive patterns.
- Method → ../methods/counter-metrics.md.

### Need leadership to act on a risk? (mock up a bad headline)
- Step: mock up bad press on own product → show leadership, ask how to avoid it → add concrete dos + don'ts.
- Why: mocked-up bad headline reaches executives faster than strategy slides.
- Deception rarely converts short-term spikes into loyal customers.

### Team habits: does the team respect its own attention? (model healthy habits inside the team)
- Do: respect colleagues' calendars.
- Do: no laptops/phones in meetings.
- Do: no messages/pings after working hours.
- Do: hold retrospectives on workload.
- Do: leaders adopt these habits first.
- Do: re-educate device habits; don't rely on detoxing.
- Why: team disrespecting own members' attention won't defend its users' attention.

### Should this be built at all? (say why, say no, reflect on drift)
- Do: ask "why are we making this?" = core designer skill.
- Do: when relaying product's negative impact, bring way to remove it.
- If no way to remove negative impacts → block shipping.
- Step: every few months, ask whether team's ethical goalposts moved.
- Why: designers = gatekeepers owing counsel, not only labour. Ethics erodes in small steps, each looking defensible.

### About to raise an alarm? (prepare collective backing)
1. Read company policy.
2. Know available escalation + whistleblowing resources.
3. Find allies before raising alarm about harm.
4. Favour collective statements over individual ones.
- Why: individuals fighting well-resourced employers alone lose.

### Repeating a founding story or headline number? (verify parables + statistics)
- Do: check founding anecdotes + headline numbers before repeating.
- Why: debunked story undermines teller's credibility.
