# Humane-design ideas

Key concepts, claims + dates of humane design, by theme. Check here before citing/applying humane-design idea.

## Use when
- Before citing humane-design idea, claim or date.
- Six themes: calm technology + attention · persuasion, deception + defaults · surveillance, data + algorithms · augmentation, empowerment + user-friendliness · inclusion + accessibility · ethics, responsibility + foresight.
- Reading on these ideas → timeline.md (foundational works). Vocabulary → five glossary files: glossary-a-c.md, glossary-d-f.md, glossary-g-m.md, glossary-n-p.md, glossary-r-z.md.

## Calm technology + attention
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Calm technology|From computing research lab, mid-1990s; technology moves between periphery + center of person's attention|
|Ubiquitous computing|From same research-lab work as calm technology|
|1991 proposition on invisible technology|Technology runs deepest once everyday life absorbs it so fully nobody notices it any more|
|Calm-technology principles|2015: turned into 8 principles; 2018: keynote extended them to forecast wave of 50 billion networked objects; has calm-design checklist + scorecard|
|Device etiquette|Device should whisper, inform + ask, softening signals in gradients|
|Enchanted / glanceable objects|Give value w/o demanding attention|
|Psychological hijacks|10 attention hijacks, incl. menu control, variable rewards, fear of missing out + bottomless bowls|
|Time Well Spent; the contest for primal impulses|From same attention critique as hijacks; second = products competing for attention by appealing to ever more primitive impulses|
|Protecting attention|Talks in 2014 + 2017 argued for it: day after day, small group of firms shapes where billions of people's attention goes|
|Distraction|At root, goal misalignment|
|Attentional GPS|Technology framed as attentional GPS|
|Freedom at stake|Freedom to want what one wants to want|
|Pocket slot machine|Modeled on anthropological study of slot-machine design|
|Persuasive design + children|One critique casts persuasive design as attack aimed at children|
|Consuming less technology|Essay argues for cutting technology intake + describes what designers can contribute|

- Case: motorized dangling string turning network traffic into ambient motion = canonical peripheral display.
- Case: infinite scroll invented 2006; inventor later publicly regretted its effect.
- Evidence strands: anthropological study of slot-machine design; research on shrinking attention spans + interruption cost.
- If designing interruption → weigh urgency + medium; adapt to behavior + context; enable adaptation; reduce mental cost.
- If planning release → make reduction explicit goal (fewer features, fewer demands, less interface) → each release claims smaller share of day than the one before.

## Persuasion, deception + defaults
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Dark → deceptive patterns|Term dark patterns coined 2010, now replaced by deceptive patterns; one catalog holds 400+ examples; family names incl. roach motel, sneak into basket + privacy zuckering|
|Captology|Study of computers as persuasive technology; from 1998|
|Behavior model|Behavior = motivation + ability + trigger|
|Persuasive advantages|Machines hold 6 advantages over human persuaders|
|Hook model|Trigger → action → variable reward → investment|
|Manipulation matrix|Test of whether hooking users justified|
|Principles of influence|6: reciprocity, scarcity, authority, consistency, liking, social proof|
|Nudge + choice architecture|Echo chambers + choosing not to choose = concepts from same line of work|
|Why defaults stick|Prospect theory + loss aversion explain why people rarely leave defaults|
|Convenience vs stated preferences|Convenience overrides people's stated preferences + breeds monopoly|
|Frictionless baseline|Frictionless usability = baseline; intentional friction = exception to it|
|Exploited temptation|Catalog of ways interaction design exploits temptation|
|White-hat UX|Scorecards + risk matrices moving business off deceptive patterns; from practical book on design-work ethics|
|Shades of influence|Influence sorted into white-hat, grey + black-hat|

- Evidence: defaults research: opt-out roughly doubles organ-donor consent vs opt-in.
- If judging influence tactic → ask: whose goal does it serve? Would person accept it if they knew how it works?

## Surveillance, data + algorithms
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Surveillance capitalism|Treats behavior as raw material; trades in behavioral futures markets|
|Big Other|Concept of surveillance-capitalism critique|
|Luxury surveillance|Tracking people pay for; structurally kin to parolee's ankle monitor|
|Filter bubble|Concept from 2011|
|Algorithmic harm + encoded bias|Strand of humane-design thought; one named concept = New Jim Code|

- Evidence: 2022 paper: filter bubble sits partly in people's own heads; rigidity driven by epistemic discomfort + context collapse, not by ranking alone.
- Case: The Markup, nonprofit newsroom, publishes real-time website privacy inspector revealing trackers, fingerprinting + session recording.
- Case: insiders going public on AI + big-tech harms paid personal price; one video interview series episode tells their stories.
- Do: treat privacy as positive design opportunity. Avoid: treating it as compliance burden.

## Augmentation, empowerment + user-friendliness
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Intelligence augmentation (1962)|Raise human capability by improving whole system: language, artifacts, methods + training|
|Bootstrapping|Concept from same augmentation work|
|User friendly|Idea has its own written history|
|Anticipation|Machines anticipating us carry hidden cost|
|Utility vs empowerment|Utility does not equal empowerment|
|Happy-path design|Business-centered|
|User-centered design; affordances|Foundational concepts|
|Meaning-driven innovation|Named concept of this line|
|Principles of good design|10 principles; 11th proposed for connected age: respect users + do no harm|
|Menu-control critique|Underpinned by human values + choicemaking|

- Do: if momentary want + person's deeper aspirations pull apart → serve aspirations.
- Of all design goals this century, resilience comes first.
- Language: later call → drop label user; speak of people themselves. Fuller case for retiring label: name people + purposes they arrived with, not their product consumption → widens what team counts as success.

## Inclusion + accessibility
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Disability|Mismatch between person + their environment|
|Exclusion|Designed, so it can get un-designed|
|Inclusive design practice at Microsoft|Recognize exclusion; learn from diversity; solve for one, extend to many. Uses persona spectrum: permanent, temporary + situational limitations|
|Universal design|Later reworked into inclusive + accessible design|
|Exclusive design|Build tailor-made experiences w/ one disabled person → flips ability bias|
|Case for inclusion|Can get pitched on three grounds: fresh-idea source, company's way to meet its duty to society, wider audience product can serve|
|Design justice|Design led by marginalized communities|

- Accessibility's audience = everybody, not minority.
- Diversity + ethics belong together w/ accessibility.
- Wrong: when inclusive work feels slow, slowness = cost inclusion imposes → Right: it = team learning new skill.

## Ethics, responsibility + foresight
|Idea|What it holds|
|---|---|
|Designer responsibility|Designers contributed to damage now afflicting world + must answer for it; ethics charter for profession; case for licensing designers + designers' unions|
|Designer as gatekeeper|Owes counsel + the word no|
|Consequence ethics|Ethics of technology's future consequences; 3 lenses: duty, outcome, virtue; concepts incl. moral imagination; redefines who counts as stakeholder|
|../methods/three-gate-check.md|Named concept of this line|
|../methods/four-quadrants-of-responsibility.md|4 quadrants of design responsibility, spanning pixel to ecosystem + individual to society|
|Ethics checkpoints|In order: Evaluate → Forecast → Monitor|
|Baseline of good design|Autonomy, transparency + safety|
|Ethical design hierarchy|Human rights → human effort → human experience|
|Personhood online|Essay: what personhood amounts to once life runs through networked technology|
|Provocation cards|Help anticipate product's impact|
|../methods/futures-wheel.md|From early 1970s|
|../methods/premortem.md + prospective hindsight|Paired foresight concepts|
|Artifacts have politics; materialized morality|Morality built into artifacts; one article probes whether infrastructure can carry moral fault in its own right|
|Inadvertent algorithmic cruelty|Named concept of this line|
|Checklists|Guard against expert error under pressure; idea borrowed for bias checklists|
|Design manifesto of 1964, renewed in 2000|Redirects design away from pure consumer persuasion|

- Do: favor transparency over delight; make data collection opt-in.
- Design for safety: if product abuse possible, it will happen.
- Abusability: ask of every feature how easily one person could turn it against another; give abusability same standing as usability.
- Harassment, stalking + coercive control = foreseeable outcomes of ordinary features → mitigate in design, not in policy afterwards.
- Wrong: edge case = outside scope → Right: calling something edge case defines limits of what you care about.
- Symptom: what marginalized people go through on platform = early sign hate has found home there.
- Favor deliberate pace + repairing broken things over speed.
- Behavior change should serve person's own goals, w/ check against going too far.
- Design against cognitive bias in users + designers alike.
